## 1. Paraguay — Paraguay’s Santiago Peña is removed and confined to his home as Pedro Alliana takes over

**The Senate on Friday removed the head of state, brought down by the collapse of Ueno bank, and his vice-president was sworn in at once. Prosecutors have ordered the former president to stay at home in an inquiry into suspected breach of trust and influence-peddling.**

On Friday 6 November Paraguay’s Senate removed President Santiago Peña from office, by the unanimous vote of the 44 senators present according to the Associated Press, a day after the lower house impeached him, and Vice-President Pedro Alliana was sworn in in Asunción to serve out a term that runs until August 2028. Prosecutors promptly ordered the former head of state to remain at home, suspecting him of breach of trust, attempted fraud and influence-peddling in the affair of Ueno bank, seized on 3 November, where the public pension fund had placed $355m beyond legal limits. Mr Peña, whose resignation Congress refused and who thereby forfeits the life seat in the Senate promised to former presidents, appeared before prosecutors on Saturday morning; they have also issued an arrest warrant for the former central-bank governor, Carlos Carvallo.

**The number :** 44 — senators voted to remove Santiago Peña

### A bank for two-thirds of adults

The scale of the crisis reflects Ueno’s place in Paraguayans’ lives. According to the Associated Press, some 3m customers, nearly two-thirds of the country’s adults, lost access to their savings and cards when it was seized, and more than 1m people had opened their first account there. The fifth-largest bank by deposits, it held more than $800m of public money. Depositors are starting to be repaid, beginning with balances below 10m guaranís, about $1,600, while the state guarantee covers up to some $37,000. Prosecutors also accuse Mr Peña, a former shareholder in the parent company, of appointing a former Ueno executive to run the central bank.

## 3. Ukraine — Death toll from the Komyshuvakha strike passes 50 in Ukraine ahead of talks in Abu Dhabi

**Ukraine’s emergency services on Friday raised to at least 50 the death toll from the bombing of an aid centre in the Zaporizhzhia region. On the front line, Russia is barely advancing, according to the Institute for the Study of War.**

On Friday 6 November Ukraine’s State Emergency Service raised to at least 50 the death toll from the Russian strike of 2 November on an aid-distribution centre in a former school in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, where dozens of people were still missing. Volodymyr Zelensky describes a double strike with glide bombs, which the UN human-rights monitoring mission says probably violated international law, at the end of a week in which Russia launched 2,346 drones and more than 80 missiles at Ukraine, according to Al Jazeera. On the battlefield Russia has taken only 117 square kilometres in ten months, according to the Institute for the Study of War, while Kyiv has targeted eight Russian refineries in seven days, ahead of three-way talks scheduled for 10 and 11 November in Abu Dhabi.

**The number :** 117 km² — taken by Russia in ten months, according to the ISW

### A war of attrition that has almost stalled

According to the Institute for the Study of War, Russia has advanced by an average of 0.38 square kilometres a day in 2026, against 13.25 in 2025, and it even lost ground in September and October. Ukraine’s general staff puts the number of Russian soldiers killed or seriously wounded between January and October at 381,300, a figure that cannot be verified. With no breakthroughs, each side is targeting the other’s economy: Moscow strikes Ukraine’s power grid as winter approaches, Kyiv hits Russian refineries from Yaroslavl to Ryazan. According to the Financial Times, cited by Al Jazeera, four tankers loaded with Russian diesel failed to find buyers in America despite the deal struck on 9 October between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin; the Kremlin denies this.

## 4. Israel — Avigdor Liberman opens the door to the ultra-Orthodox on the eve of Isaac Herzog’s choice

**The leader of Yisrael Beiteinu says he is ready to govern with the ultra-Orthodox parties but rejects any government reliant on the Arab parties. Israel’s president names the leader tasked with forming a government on Sunday.**

On the eve of Sunday’s nomination of the leader tasked with forming a government, Avigdor Liberman, who leads Yisrael Beiteinu, said he was open to a coalition with the ultra-Orthodox parties and to compromises on conscription, while vowing to vote against any government dependent on the Arab parties. Gadi Eisenkot, recommended by 67 of the 120 members of the Knesset against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu, owes that lead to the 19 members from Ra’am and the United List, with whom three of the four parties in his bloc refuse to sit, though the bloc has only 48 seats. The Democrats’ leader, Yair Golan, speaks of “progress”, while the Likud lawmaker Miri Regev calls the rival alliance a “bloc of disaster”; the new Knesset will be sworn in on 10 November and will elect a successor to its speaker, Amir Ohana.

**The number :** 67 — members of the Knesset recommend Gadi Eisenkot for prime minister

### No majority without the Arab parties

The Knesset’s arithmetic traps Gadi Eisenkot in a dilemma. Without Ra’am and the United List, his 48-member bloc falls well short of the 61 seats required; with them, he runs into the refusal of most of his allies, including Avigdor Liberman, who demands a 61-member coalition without the Arab parties and rejects any minority government supported from outside. His overture to United Torah Judaism, which recommended no one with its seven seats, does not close the gap. Mr Eisenkot also sets conditions of his own: he wants Hamas recognised as a terrorist organisation, which Ra’am’s leader, Mansour Abbas, has not done, though he says he favours its disarmament. Mr Liberman, for his part, denies any rotation agreement with Mr Eisenkot.

## 5. DR Congo — Two new vaccines tested in DR Congo against an Ebola outbreak that has killed over 5,000

**The WHO has added two vaccines designed against the Bundibugyo strain to the trial under way in Ituri. The outbreak, the worst in the country’s history, has more than 10,500 confirmed cases.**

On Wednesday 4 November the World Health Organisation announced that two vaccines designed against the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, one developed by the University of Oxford and the Serum Institute of India, the other by Moderna, would join the clinical trial under way in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo. Declared in mid-May, the outbreak, the worst in the country’s history, had more than 10,500 confirmed cases and 5,126 deaths by 1 November, according to WHO figures cited by the UN, and no vaccine or treatment is licensed against this strain, which kills more than half of those infected. The Tumaini trial, launched on 23 October in Bunia, capital of Ituri province, vaccinates the contacts of patients using the so-called ring method, but its results are not expected before early 2027, while the virus has already reached Uganda and Kenya.

**The number :** 5,126 — Ebola deaths in DR Congo by 1 November, according to the WHO

### A first against the Bundibugyo strain

This is the first time that vaccines designed specifically against the Bundibugyo virus have been tested during an outbreak, according to the WHO. Until now the trial relied on Ervebo, licensed against a different strain, Zaire. The Oxford and Moderna candidates, recommended by an independent advisory group on the strength of preclinical and early clinical data, will join four vaccination “rings” already in place around confirmed cases, led by Congo’s National Institute for Biomedical Research with Médecins Sans Frontières and Africa CDC. Another trial, launched in July, is assessing treatments, including a monoclonal antibody and remdesivir. The WHO’s director-general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, warns, however, that vaccines alone will not end the outbreak.
