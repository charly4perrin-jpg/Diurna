In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot wins the backing of most MPs against Benjamin Netanyahu

Abandoned by the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism party, Benjamin Netanyahu gathers only 46 recommendations, against 67 for his rival, who is backed by the Arab parties. In Gaza, a year after the ceasefire, Israeli strikes still claim lives almost every day.

Edition of 5 Nov 2026Running story · Israel and GazaWritten by an AI from the sources cited · How it’s made

At the end of consultations held by the president, Isaac Herzog, on 4 and 5 November, 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members recommended Gadi Eisenkot to form a government: the opposition’s 48, joined by the two Arab parties, Raam and the Joint List, with 19 seats between them. Benjamin Netanyahu, whose camp won 53 seats on 27 October, now musters only 46, after the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism declined to recommend anyone without guarantees on military-service exemptions for yeshiva students. The head of state is expected within days to ask Mr Eisenkot, who says he wants to form a “Zionist government”, to build a coalition within 28 days, extendable by 14, with the Joint List pledging to back him from outside government.

In Gaza, a truce that offers no protection

The ceasefire brokered by Washington took effect in October 2025, but nearly 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since, according to Gaza’s health ministry, which counts more than 74,600 dead in all since the war began in October 2023. The Israeli army says it is targeting members of Hamas, including commanders involved in the 7 October attack. Donald Trump’s 20-point peace plan, which would hand the territory’s administration to a “Board of Peace” and its security to an international force, has largely stalled; its envoys, Nickolay Mladenov and Tony Blair, are in Israel this week to try to revive it.

Key points
The weight of the Arab parties
They represent Israel’s Arab citizens, about a fifth of the population. In 2021 the Raam party joined a governing coalition for the first time; this time both Raam and the Joint List are recommending Gadi Eisenkot, though the Joint List rules out joining his government.
What happens next
The president must now ask one of the leaders to form a government, with the new Knesset holding its opening session on 10 November. If no coalition emerges within the legal deadlines, voters would be called back to the polls.
