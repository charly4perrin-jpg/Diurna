## 1. Israel — Isaac Herzog asks Gadi Eisenkot, not Benjamin Netanyahu, to form Israel’s next government

**On Saturday evening Israel’s president gave the leader of Yashar, backed by 67 members of the Knesset, the job of building a coalition. Benjamin Netanyahu accuses his rival of wanting to govern with “supporters of terrorism”.**

On the evening of Saturday 7 November Israel’s president, Isaac Herzog, announced that he would ask Gadi Eisenkot, a former chief of staff who leads the Yashar party, to form the next government, with the mandate to be handed over formally on Sunday at the presidential residence in Jerusalem. The former general was recommended by 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members, including the Arab parties, against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu, but his own bloc holds only 48 seats and several of his allies refuse to depend on Ra’am and the United List. Mr Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14, to muster at least 61 members, while the outgoing prime minister is calling for a “national unity government” that would include his far-right allies and the ultra-Orthodox parties.

**The number :** 28 days — to form a coalition, extendable by a further 14

### A majority still to be found

Mr Eisenkot’s nomination does not solve the Knesset’s arithmetic. To reach 61 seats he must either lean on the 19 Arab members, which the more right-wing of his allies reject, or win over the ultra-Orthodox. Avigdor Liberman said on Friday that he was ready for “painful compromises” on the conscription of yeshiva students, and United Torah Judaism, which recommended no one, has floated a partnership with him. According to the Times of Israel, the likeliest outcome remains a minority government supported from outside, which Ahmad Tibi of the United List also says he expects. Mr Eisenkot has set his conditions: recognition of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, compulsory service for all and the designation of Hamas as a terrorist organisation.

## 2. Iran — Iran lifts the range limit on its missiles after fresh American strikes in Hormuz

**The Revolutionary Guards say they no longer observe a 2,000km ceiling on their ballistic missiles. Washington has set up a force to hunt down Iranian arms smuggling, as advisers urge Donald Trump to resume the war.**

On Saturday 7 November the spokesman for the Revolutionary Guards, General Hossein Mohebbi, said in Tehran that the 2,000km limit Iran had imposed on the range of its ballistic missiles had been lifted, a day after American strikes on air defences on Qeshm island. The announcement, which does not say whether Iran already has longer-range missiles, came alongside a statement attributed to the Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, promising a response “without geographical limits” to any American or Israeli threat. According to NBC, advisers have urged Donald Trump to resume large-scale operations before the new Congress sits in January, and America’s Central Command has announced a force to cut the land and Caspian routes by which Iran obtains weapons.

**The number :** > $25bn — of Iranian crude kept off export markets by the American blockade

### Squeezing the back routes

With the force dubbed Iron Gate, based in Tampa, Florida, Washington is extending its naval blockade to Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea. Its commander, Admiral Brad Cooper, says it will further curb Iran’s ability to finance terrorism. According to a Reuters investigation cited by the Times of Israel, an Iranian network has since January 2025 imported at least $2bn of equipment, explosives and lorries across the Caspian from Russia, by road from Central Asia and by air from China. The Revolutionary Guards are answering with escalation: a range of 2,000km already covers the Arabian peninsula, the Levant, most of Egypt and part of Greece.

## 3. Ukraine — Russia fires 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv three days before talks in Abu Dhabi

**Overnight on 6-7 November Russia targeted the capital’s power grid and damaged the North Bridge over the Dnipro. Volodymyr Zelensky accuses Moscow of preparing for negotiations “by launching ballistic missiles”.**

Overnight on Friday 6 and Saturday 7 November Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and its region, only four of which were shot down for want of Patriot interceptors, according to President Volodymyr Zelensky, along with 172 drones across 13 regions, wounding at least nine people, including a child. The strikes hit the substations linking the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants to the capital, leaving parts of the city without electricity, heating and water, and damaged the North Bridge, which Moscow says it was aiming at. Ukrainian, Russian and American delegations are due to meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday 10 November for a fourth round of talks brokered by Washington, as the death toll from the strike on Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, reaches 55.

**The number :** 36 — ballistic missiles fired at Kyiv and its region

### Winter as a weapon

With no breakthroughs at the front, Moscow is concentrating its fire on energy as winter approaches. According to Ukraine’s energy ministry, Saturday’s strikes damaged the lines carrying power from the nuclear plants to Kyiv and central Ukraine, causing outages in the Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Kharkiv, Donetsk and Dnipropetrovsk regions on top of scheduled blackouts. Kyiv’s mayor, Vitali Klitschko, calls the situation “extremely difficult”. Volodymyr Zelensky says North Korean missiles were used and is pressing Washington for more interceptors. Kyiv is hitting back at Russia’s economy: a refinery in Saratov and a factory in Oryol were struck, according to the authorities and Russian media.

## 4. Ireland — Hoax bomb threats spread to European newsrooms after targeting dozens of flights

**Ireland’s public broadcaster, RTÉ, evacuated its offices on Friday after a threatening email, as did Bulgaria’s Nova TV. Nearly 30 flights have been targeted since Tuesday, and no explosives have been found.**

On Friday 6 November emails signed by one “Mikhail Andropov” and headed “Message to the West” threatened Ireland’s public broadcaster, RTÉ, which evacuated its offices and suspended programming, as well as Bulgaria’s Nova TV, while Sky reported a threat in Britain. The messages extend a wave of alerts that has disrupted nearly 30 flights in Europe since Tuesday, prompting diversions and fighter-jet escorts, though no explosives have been found, according to Reuters, which could not establish where the emails came from. No country has named a culprit, but Spain’s foreign minister, José Manuel Albares, has denounced a “hybrid aggression”, and NATO has ordered air patrols near the border between Hungary and Romania.

**The number :** ≈ 30 — flights targeted by hoax threats since 3 November

### Suspicions of a hybrid operation

Security experts quoted by Reuters see the hallmarks of a coordinated “hybrid” attack, while not ruling out copycats or personal grievances. The emails sent on Thursday to Rome and Milan airports claimed to come from “Male State”, a far-right group banned in Russia whose founder, the blogger Vladislav Pozdnyakov, says it no longer exists and denies any involvement. Many European officials link the disruption to Europe’s support for Ukraine and accuse Moscow of running a destabilisation campaign, which Russia denies. The wave adds to a string of sabotage attributed to hybrid operations, targeting German substations, Baltic defence sites and Leipzig airport.

## 5. Haiti — Haiti’s 400 Mawozo gang reaches out to the state as Salvadoran troops close in

**Wilson Joseph’s gang says it has contacted the national disarmament commission and wants UN mediation. The prime minister, Alix Didier Fils-Aimé, has announced the killing of a gang leader in Cité Soleil.**

On Friday 6 November the 400 Mawozo gang, led by Wilson Joseph, told the Associated Press that it had contacted Haiti’s national commission for disarmament, demobilisation and reintegration in the name of “peace” and “national reconciliation”. Accused by the UN and human-rights groups of rape, murder and kidnapping, which it denies, the gang fears an offensive by the more than 2,000 Salvadoran soldiers deployed in recent weeks, and El Salvador’s president, Nayib Bukele, has promised to destroy its leader’s house. The same day the prime minister, Alix Didier Fils-Aimé, announced the death of a gang leader in Cité Soleil, identified by police as the head of the Boston gang, while the disarmament commission says it is following the government’s road map.

**The number :** > 2,000 — Salvadoran soldiers deployed in Haiti

### El Salvador’s gamble

The arrival of Salvadoran troops has shifted the balance of power in Port-au-Prince. According to the UN, most of them operate outside the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force, which works with the police, the Haitian army and Vectus Global, a private security firm founded by Erik Prince. Their deployment followed the disappearance of four Salvadoran aviation technicians, and they were attacked on 18 October in gang-held territory. The overture by 400 Mawozo, which is asking for operations to be suspended in some areas and invokes the Geneva Conventions, looks like an attempt to protect itself. The gang says disarmament is “a process” and wants an end to “international interference” in return.
