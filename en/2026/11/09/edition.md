## 1. Israel — Gadi Eisenkot accepts the mandate in Israel and calls for a national emergency government

**The former army chief was formally asked on Sunday to form a government, with 67 recommendations but a bloc of only 48 members. He has invited Likud to join him and set four conditions for any partner.**

On Sunday 8 November, at the presidential residence in Jerusalem, Israel’s president, Isaac Herzog, formally handed Gadi Eisenkot, the leader of the Yashar party, the mandate to form the next government, a day after announcing his choice. Recommended by 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members, against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu, the former chief of staff commands a bloc of only 48 and has promised creative, unconventional political moves to build a national emergency government. He has until 6 December, extendable to 20 December, to assemble a majority, while the new members are due to be sworn in on Tuesday and Mr Netanyahu stays on as caretaker prime minister.

**The number :** 6 December — end of the initial deadline to form a coalition

### An open door for Likud

In his speech Mr Eisenkot did not mention Benjamin Netanyahu by name, but invited Likud and the Religious Zionist Party to join his coalition, saying his door was open. He set four conditions for any partner: recognising Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, respecting the values of its declaration of independence, accepting compulsory military or civilian service and committing to destroy Hamas, which he called a terrorist organisation. He made no mention of Otzma Yehudit, with which his deputy, Yoram Cohen, has ruled out governing. According to the Times of Israel, the most commonly cited arithmetic remains a 61-vote majority resting on his bloc, the seven members of United Torah Judaism and benign abstention by Shas.

## 2. Saudi Arabia — Houthi attack damages an Aramco tower in the heart of Riyadh’s financial district

**Missiles fired from Yemen were intercepted over the Saudi capital on Sunday, but debris struck a building belonging to the oil giant. The Houthi authorities accuse Saudi aircraft of killing dozens of people near Sanaa.**

Overnight on Saturday 7 and Sunday 8 November Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles fired at Riyadh, but debris blew out the windows of several floors of an Aramco building in the King Abdullah Financial District, causing no casualties, according to the energy ministry. The Iran-aligned Yemeni rebels have stepped up their fire on the kingdom’s ports and airports since September, and their spokesman, Yahya Saree, claims they also targeted Riyadh’s international airport, which the Saudi authorities have not confirmed. The Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed missile launchers in Saada and Sanaa, while the Houthi-run health ministry reports at least 33 people killed on Saturday and 40 on Sunday in Saudi strikes near the Yemeni capital.

**The number :** $107 — a barrel of Brent crude on 3 November

### Another front in a regional war

According to Reuters, the Houthi offensive against the kingdom amounts to a new front in the war that America and Israel have waged on Iran since February. After advancing towards the Bab al-Mandab strait in September, the rebels have fired a growing number of missiles and drones at Saudi infrastructure; on 10 October an attack on Riyadh’s airport killed 13 people, according to CBS News. The Houthis also claim strikes on Aramco storage tanks in Riyadh and Abqaiq, which the Institute for the Study of War has been unable to verify. Meanwhile Iran’s Revolutionary Guards have targeted ships in the Strait of Hormuz, keeping up the pressure on the world’s oil supply.

## 3. Ukraine — In Ukraine 850,000 people lose power ahead of the talks in Abu Dhabi

**Fresh Russian strikes hit the power grid on Sunday, notably in the Dnipropetrovsk region, and the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant lost its external power supply. Paris says Europeans will take part in the talks on 10 and 11 November.**

Overnight Russian strikes on Sunday 8 November left more than 850,000 people in Ukraine without electricity, over 600,000 of them in the Dnipropetrovsk region, according to the energy company DTEK, while one person was killed and three wounded in Dnipro. The Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant lost its external power and is running on diesel generators, and a drone hit the site in Komyshuvakha where an earlier strike killed at least 55 people, targeting rescuers, according to Kyiv. France’s foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, announced that France and other Europeans would join the talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, which had until now been conceived as a meeting between Washington, Moscow and Kyiv.

**The number :** > 850,000 — people left without electricity after the overnight strikes

### Winter as a weapon

Moscow is pressing on with its campaign against Ukraine’s energy grid as winter approaches. Volodymyr Zelensky says Russia fired 115 missiles, nearly 1,900 guided bombs and more than 3,000 attack drones in a single week. Russia’s defence ministry says it hit a substation supplying firms in the military-industrial complex. In the Zhytomyr region 140 apartment blocks were left without heating, and in the Kharkiv region up to 250,000 customers lost power, since restored. The loss of external power at the Zaporizhzhia plant, after the Ferosplavna line was disconnected, revives fears over the safety of Europe’s largest nuclear site, which now depends on its back-up generators.

## 4. Türkiye — COP31 opens in Antalya as the world looks set to overshoot 1.5°C

**Nearly 200 countries meet in Turkey until 20 November for the UN climate conference, presided over by Ankara with Australia leading the negotiations. Current policies point to 2.8°C of warming, according to the UN’s latest projection.**

The 31st UN climate conference opens on Monday 9 November in Antalya, Turkey, bringing together delegations from nearly 200 countries until 20 November under the presidency of Turkey’s environment minister, Murat Kurum. Scientists consider it almost certain that warming will exceed 1.5°C in 2026, and current policies point to a rise of 2.8°C by the end of the century, according to the UN’s latest projection, cited by the German news agency DPA. Australia’s climate minister, Chris Bowen, will chair the negotiations, an unprecedented division of roles, ahead of a leaders’ summit on 11 and 12 November at which markedly fewer heads of state are expected than at recent COPs.

**The number :** 2.8°C — of warming expected by 2100 under current policies

### A two-headed presidency

Turkey and Australia both bid to host the conference; the compromise reached in November 2025 gave Ankara the organisation and the action agenda, and Canberra the conduct of the negotiations. DPA notes that this novel arrangement has raised fears of friction, as the two countries’ proposals were poorly co-ordinated during the preparations. The chosen theme, implementation and acceleration, signals more modest ambitions: according to the UN’s synthesis of plans from 154 countries, national pledges would cut emissions only about 6% below 2019 levels by 2035. Adaptation finance, which COP30 in Belém promised to triple, looks set to be one of the main sticking points.

## 5. Myanmar — Myanmar’s army kills eleven people in an air strike on a school in Sagaing

**Teachers and children were among the victims of a bombing on Saturday in Kani township, according to independent Burmese media. The National Unity Government counted 641 army air strikes in October.**

At around 11.30am on Saturday 7 November an air strike by Myanmar’s army hit a school and the village of Nyaung Pin Le, in Kani township in the Sagaing region, killing eleven people, most of them teachers and children, according to independent media. The bombing is part of an air campaign that killed 212 civilians in October, 23 of them children, according to the exiled National Unity Government, nearly six years after the coup and seven months after Min Aung Hlaing was elected president. Fighting continues in Mon State, where more than 15,000 residents have fled Kyaikto township, and in Rakhine, while from 9 November the army is closing checkpoints at night in three Sagaing townships.

**The number :** 641 — army air strikes in October

### War from the air

Unable to retake large swathes of the countryside, the army relies on its air force, which the resistance has no means to counter. Sagaing, the heartland of armed opposition to the 2021 coup, is the worst-hit region: besides the school in Kani, a hospital in Kawlin township was bombed on 4 November, killing at least 12 people including a doctor, and a shelter for displaced people was hit in Wetlet. Min Aung Hlaing’s election as president in April, after a vote organised by the army, has not ended the fighting. Shells fired in Karen State have also landed in Thailand’s Tak province, damaging homes.
