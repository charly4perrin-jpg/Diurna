## 1. Iran — America strikes an Iranian missile site on Qeshm as drones target Kuwait

**A US official says the military hit a missile-launch site on the island of Qeshm on Tuesday evening, in retaliation for Iran’s attack on a base in Bahrain. Overnight, Kuwait said it was intercepting "hostile" drones.**

American forces struck a missile-launch site on the Iranian island of Qeshm, in the Strait of Hormuz, on the evening of Tuesday 17 November, according to a US official quoted by Al Jazeera, hours after the Revolutionary Guards claimed a drone attack on a radar at Sheikh Isa air base in Bahrain. The deputy governor of Hormozgan province, Ahmad Nafisi, insists that no homes or businesses were hit, while US Central Command says the drones launched at Bahrain, in the third Iranian attack on the kingdom in five days, were intercepted without causing damage. Overnight Kuwait’s army said it was repelling "hostile" drone attacks without saying where they came from, and Manama condemned Iran’s "criminal attacks", on the eve of the Shenzhen summit at which Washington wants to raise the war with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin.

**The number :** 3 — Iranian drone attacks on Bahrain in five days

### Targeted reprisals around Hormuz

Since the war began on 28 February, Tehran has sought to restrict traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries a fifth of the world’s oil, by imposing its own shipping lanes; Washington has answered with a blockade of Iranian ports and naval escorts for tankers. Friday’s attack on Bahrain was, according to AFP, the first on a Gulf state since early September. The targets chosen on both sides, a radar and a launch site, remain strictly military. Kuwait has not said where the drones that targeted it overnight came from, and no casualty figures have been released. Yet the exchanges of fire are growing more frequent as American forces build up in the region, with a third aircraft carrier, the Theodore Roosevelt, arriving on Monday.

## 2. China — Putin arrives in Shenzhen ahead of his first three-way summit with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping

**Russia’s president landed in Shenzhen on Tuesday and is due to hold talks with Xi Jinping on Wednesday before Thursday’s three-way meeting with Donald Trump. The American president flies on to China after a state visit to Manila on Wednesday.**

Vladimir Putin landed at Shenzhen’s Bao’an airport on Tuesday 17 November for his first APEC summit in person since 2017, and is due to meet Xi Jinping on Wednesday ahead of the first three-way meeting with Donald Trump, scheduled for Thursday on the sidelines of the summit. Washington wants to discuss cutting nuclear arsenals, since the last Russian-American nuclear-arms treaty expired in February, as well as the Iran war, while Ukraine’s place on the agenda remains unclear, Donald Trump having said on Monday that he would raise it despite signals to the contrary from the White House. Before leaving the Philippines, the American president was received on Wednesday for a state visit by Ferdinand Marcos Jr at Malacañang Palace, where he said he was "not concerned" about tensions in the South China Sea, and he has not ruled out seeing Kim Jong Un in China.

**The number :** 2017 — Vladimir Putin’s last APEC summit in person

### Three powers, three agendas

The idea of a three-way meeting was floated during a telephone call on 9 October, according to the Kyiv Post. For Donald Trump it will be a third in-person meeting with Xi Jinping this year, after his visit to China in May and the Chinese leader’s trip to America in September; he has not seen Vladimir Putin since their Alaska summit in August 2025. The nuclear question shows how far apart the three are: Washington wants to bring Beijing into arms control, while China refuses to negotiate over its smaller arsenal until the other two have cut theirs. The Kremlin hopes for a joint communiqué, and a one-to-one meeting between the Russian and American presidents is being prepared, according to the Kyiv Post.

## 3. Israel — Board of Peace warns that the Gaza ceasefire is at risk of collapse

**In a report delivered to the UN Security Council on Tuesday, the body overseeing the truce accuses Israel of blocking the transitional civilian administration. It argues that a ceasefire producing "nothing visible" cannot last indefinitely.**

The Board of Peace, set up in 2025 at Donald Trump’s initiative to oversee the truce between Israel and Hamas, delivered a report to the United Nations Security Council on Tuesday 17 November warning that the deal risks collapse unless it is implemented immediately, according to the Associated Press. The document accuses Israel of preventing the technocratic civilian administration from taking office in Gaza and of failing to decide on the return of a civilian police force, which is delaying the international stabilisation force, and also cites Israeli strikes and Hamas’s military activity. It asks the Security Council to press both sides, warns that if the plan fails there is "no alternative roadmap", and calls for shelters and winter supplies to be let in on a large scale for a population facing "another winter in tents".

**The number :** 6 months — since the Board of Peace’s first report, in May

### The missing link: a stabilisation force

The whole plan rests on a sequence that the report describes as stalled: without a civilian police force there can be no international stabilisation force, and without that no Israeli withdrawal, no disarmament of Hamas and no reconstruction. As early as May, the Board of Peace’s director-general, Nickolay Mladenov, blamed the impasse on the Islamist movement’s disarmament; six months on, the diagnosis is unchanged. The report also stresses the humanitarian emergency: restrictions and a shortage of funding are hampering preparations for winter, and the body is seeking permits for pumps, generators and heavy machinery. The decision rests largely with Israel, where Gadi Eisenkot was asked on 7 November to form a government after October’s election.

## 4. Ukraine — Ukraine launches a record drone attack on Russia, hitting a refinery in Bashkortostan

**Moscow says it intercepted 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight from Monday to Tuesday, a record since 2022. Fires were reported at a Gazprom petrochemical complex in Salavat, and an arch of the Crimean bridge has collapsed.**

Russia’s defence ministry said on Tuesday 17 November that it had intercepted 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight over Russia and annexed Crimea, a record since the invasion began that eclipses the 1,336 claimed the previous night. Fires were reported at the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat complex in Bashkortostan, more than 1,200 kilometres from the border, which regional authorities say is intact, while Ukraine’s security service claims to have destroyed a road arch of the Crimean bridge in a seven-stage operation. The same night, Russian missiles hit Kyiv’s North and South bridges and energy facilities, cutting power in eight regions according to the deputy energy minister, Artem Nekrasov, two days before a summit in Shenzhen at which the war’s place remains uncertain.

**The number :** 1,441 — Ukrainian drones intercepted in one night, according to Moscow

### A war of bridges and refineries

Volodymyr Zelensky presents the strike on the Kerch bridge as a response to Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure, including bridges in Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kyiv and Odesa. The Salavat complex, already hit on 8 October according to Ukraine’s general staff, produces petrol, diesel and jet fuel and supplies the Russian army, according to the Kyiv Post. Both sides deny deliberately targeting civilians. These very long-range attacks continue despite the first talks in nine months, held last week in Abu Dhabi under American auspices. The Kremlin’s spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, said a plan to repair the bridge would be drawn up.

## 5. Haiti — UN counts more than 4,500 killed in Haiti’s gang violence since January

**The UN human rights office recorded 4,595 deaths between January and October and urges the forces fighting the gangs to limit their use of lethal force. In Port-au-Prince, hundreds of people marched in support of Salvadoran troops.**

The UN human rights office said in Geneva on Tuesday 17 November that 4,595 people had been killed and 2,050 wounded in Haiti between 1 January and 31 October, including at least 514 killed in security-force operations against gangs between July and September. The high commissioner, Volker Türk, urged national and international forces, including the UN-authorised Gang Suppression Force, to use lethal force only when "unavoidable", as El Salvador has deployed more than 2,000 troops since October, mostly outside that framework. Hundreds of Haitians marched on Tuesday, notably in Port-au-Prince, in support of the deployment, which San Salvador says will end once four Salvadorans abducted on 18 October are found; the leader of the 400 Mawozo gang, Wilson Joseph, claims without evidence that they have been killed.

**The number :** 4,595 — people killed in Haiti from January to October

### Civilians caught in the war on gangs

The quarterly report of the UN Integrated Office in Haiti shows that the security response now weighs heavily in the toll: more than half of those killed or wounded between July and September fell in ground or air operations against gangs, sometimes conducted with a private military company using drones. More than 16% of the victims of these operations had no link to the gangs, and 44 extrajudicial executions or attempts involving the police were documented, against 14 the previous quarter. The gangs, for their part, are stepping up reprisals against residents suspected of co-operating with the security forces, and forcibly recruiting children. The Gang Suppression Force, which arrived in April, is still not fully deployed.
